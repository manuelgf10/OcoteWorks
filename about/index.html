02 · The name

Why Ocote?

ocote/o·KO·teh/
From Nahuatl ocotl. A name for resin-rich Mexican pine and its wood, traditionally used to start fires.
Ocotepec/o·ko·teh·PEK/
From Nahuatl, meaning “on the hill of the ocotes.” The town where I was born and raised.

I grew up in Ocotepec, surrounded by pine forests.

As a kid, I loved wandering through the woods, discovering new paths, and wondering what lay beyond the next hill. That’s where my curiosity for nature, exploration, and adventure began.

Over the years, that same curiosity grew into a passion for designing, building, and creating things.

OcoteWorks is a reflection of those roots. A connection between where I come from, what I love doing, and the desire to keep going further.

Deep roots.
An inner fire.

Rooted in Mexico. Made to go further.

I studied Industrial Design at Universidad Iberoamericana, but my curiosity has always gone beyond what a product looks like. I’ve always wanted to understand how things work, how they’re built, and how they can be made better.

My professional journey began in 2015 at Mosaicos Venecianos, where I first worked alongside lathes, milling machines, and industrial equipment. Later, at IKTA, I explored the relationship between design and manufacturing through CAD, CAM, and metal fabrication. At Nova Pack, designing plastic packaging and pharmaceutical containers, I learned to program CNC machining and to speak the language of mechanical drawings, while working hands-on with injection and blow molds.

Since 2020, I’ve worked in medical device development at Bioana, where engineering, quality, and precision are essential. Managing the prototyping lab has given me the opportunity to work on both sides of product development: designing components and operating the equipment that brings them to life.

OcoteWorks brings those experiences together with my personal interests in motorcycles, mechanical systems, and the outdoors.

It’s my independent workshop. A place to explore ideas, solve problems, and turn curiosity into things you can actually build, use, and take further.
