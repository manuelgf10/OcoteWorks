I trained as an industrial designer at the Universidad Iberoamericana. Over time my work kept moving toward the same question: not only what a product should be, but how it gets made.

My first industrial job, in 2015 at Mosaicos Venecianos, put me next to lathes, milling machines and equipment maintenance. At IKTA, from 2019 to 2020, I worked on steel structures and decorative architectural elements, moving between CAD, CAM and visualization.

Since 2020 I’ve developed medical devices at Bioana, in an ISO 13485 environment where decisions are documented and parts have to be made reliably. Since 2022 I also manage the prototyping lab. Designing the parts and running the equipment that makes them has taught me more about manufacturability than either role would alone.

OcoteWorks is my independent workshop, where that background meets motorcycles, mechanical systems and the outdoors. It is not an extension of Bioana, and it isn’t a medical device company.

The name comes from ocote, the resin-rich pine splinter used to start a fire in the mountains: small, resourceful and reliable when it counts. And from Ocotepec, the town where I’m from.
