Keep it looking like a dirt bike

How the OW-02 started: a tail rack for the Ténéré 700 designed to carry just enough without changing its silhouette.

By Manuel García

There’s something I really like about the Ténéré 700: from the side it still reads as a big dirt bike. Long flat seat, tall slim tail, nothing extra. It looks made to leave the pavement.

The trouble starts when you want to carry things. Almost every tail rack out there is wide, tall and heavy. Add one, then a top case, and bit by bit the bike stops looking like what it is: it ends up looking like a bike that never leaves the pavement.

I wanted the opposite: a rack you barely notice, but that’s there when you need to strap something down.

Three rules

Before drawing anything I set myself three rules:

  • Respect the silhouette. The rack has to follow the line of the seat and tail, not stick out of it.
  • Carry close to the center. The lower and further forward the load sits, the less you feel it, especially off-road.
  • Keep it light. If the point is to keep the dirt-bike character, it makes no sense to hang kilos of steel on it.

Notebook first

I started by hand. I like sketching before opening CAD because it forces you to think about the whole idea, not the details. Almost everything was already on this page: a single folded sheet, side arms that bolt to the bike and a rear plate with slots for straps.

The questions at the bottom of the page were the important ones. And you can’t answer those on paper.

Cardboard before aluminum

Before cutting metal, I laser cut it in cardboard and bolted it to the bike with the factory screws. It’s a cheap, fast test, and it answers things a screen gets wrong: how the proportion looks against the seat, how much it shows over the taillight and whether the slots land where the straps will actually go.

Seeing the part on the bike, even in cardboard, changes the conversation. A render floating on a grey background is one thing; the rack next to a bike with dust on the seat is another. From three-quarters, like in the photo at the top, the rack disappears into the bike’s line. That’s what I was after.

One piece

The final version is a single sheet of 6061 aluminum, laser cut and folded. No welds, no joints: fewer parts, less weight and fewer places for something to work loose. The slots get CNC-rounded edges so they won’t cut webbing, and the finish is powder coat in matte black or matte white.

What’s next

Next is the aluminum prototype and testing it where it matters: dirt roads, vibration and rain. Before making the first batch I want to know how many people want it, so I opened a list. If you’re interested, sign up on the OW-02 page: nothing is charged and you’ll hear first.

I’ll keep posting here, mistakes included.
